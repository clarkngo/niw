What's genuinely strong

Real credential

Amanda "Mandy" Nease — 13 years as a USCIS adjudicator

Senior Immigration Attorney and Director of Professional Development. Spent 13 years at the Nebraska Service Center personally reviewing EB‑2 NIW petitions and training officers on adjudication standards. This is a genuine, uncommon asset for case strategy — not just marketing copy.

Fair term

RFE response included at no extra fee

Contract §9: if USCIS issues a Request for Evidence, the firm responds without charging an additional attorney fee. Not every firm includes this.

What's worth real caution

Concern — §11

The firm claims permanent ownership of your own case materials

"...drafted recommendation letters, endeavor and personal statement, and drafted petition letter is attorney work product and the sole property of Attorney at ALL TIMES even after the termination of the representation."

This names content about your own life and endeavor, not just internal firm notes. Practically: if you ever switch counsel, you may not be free to bring your own drafted materials without the firm's written consent. Ask them directly, in writing, what this means if you terminate and hire someone else.

Concern — §6

15 days late → 3%/month interest and work stops entirely

Over a 16‑month installment plan, a single delayed payment doesn't just cost a fee — per firm policy, all work on the case stops until you catch up. Worth stress‑testing against your actual income situation on F‑1 status before committing to the schedule.

Caution

Pricing sits above typical market range

$16,200 attorney fee (discounted tier) vs. a general market range of roughly $4,000–$15,000, with most successful cases reportedly landing $8,000–$14,000 total. Not disqualifying for a high‑volume, credentialed shop — but it's a premium, not an average price.

Caution

Contract reads as templated, not bespoke

§4 ("Spouse as Principal Applicant") is boilerplate that doesn't fit this situation — the spouse already holds a green card. Its presence suggests a standard-form agreement rather than one drafted around the specific facts.

Employee signal

Attorney pay runs ~33% below market

Glassdoor: average immigration attorney salary $119,802/yr vs. ~$159,810 US average for the role. Combined with the claimed case volume (2,000–2,500 approvals across roughly 3 years), this points to a high‑throughput model — each attorney likely carries a heavier caseload than a boutique shop, which is exactly why getting specifics in writing matters more here, not less.

The sales process, not just the contract

Pattern

Urgency appears twice, substance appears never

The intake call included a discount that "expires end of month." A later WhatsApp message announced a "green light from the legal team" and pushed to sign "as soon as possible" — while citing only generic strengths ("awards, publications, presentations, 3 masters degrees") that don't reflect the specific weaknesses already identified in this file (thin citation count, single‑institution letters). Neither message mentioned the one legal question that actually gates the case: INA 245(k).

Takunda, who ran the intake call and sent that message, is a client liaison — a sales/intake role, not an attorney. That's normal at a firm this size, but it means "green light from legal" should be treated as a sales summary until a licensed attorney says the same thing directly, in writing.

Bottom line

Not a scam, not a firm to walk away from on reputation alone — real credentials (especially Mandy Nease), no bar discipline found, legitimate scale. But it is a high‑volume operation priced at the top of the market, with a sales process so far built on urgency and generic reassurance rather than case‑specific analysis, and a contract with at least one term (§11) worth pushing back on before signing. None of this rules it out. It does mean the four questions above should get written answers first — not "we'll figure it out once you're on board."
